Introduction

Kidney disease is a heterogeneous group of acute and chronic disorders characterized by structural or functional impairment of the kidneys that disrupts filtration, fluid balance, electrolyte homeostasis, acid-base regulation, and endocrine function (Disease code: kidney disease). In the analysis of the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project data, circulating levels of 1077 plasma proteins were found to be significantly associated with kidney disease, based on 47,610 controls and 3,149 cases, after application of Bonferroni correction at P < 0.000000001. Understanding the biological mechanisms represented by these 1077 disease-associated proteins is essential for clarifying the molecular basis of kidney injury and progression, because kidney disease arises through the interaction of inflammatory activation, vascular dysfunction, extracellular matrix remodeling, metabolic stress, and impairment of epithelial and glomerular homeostasis. To define these mechanisms systematically, we performed a comprehensive functional enrichment analysis on the 1077 proteins, screening biological pathways from multiple resources, including Gene Ontology Molecular Function, Gene Ontology Biological Process, Gene Ontology Cellular Component, Reactome, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes. We identified 111 significantly enriched biological pathways (Table 1). These enrichments reveal a molecular landscape strongly centered on innate immune and inflammatory processes, extracellular matrix degradation and remodeling, cytoskeletal and endosomal organization, calcium- and lipid-related molecular functions, and broader pathways linked to tissue injury, vascular regulation, and metabolic disturbance. Notably, some of the strongest signals include neutrophil degranulation with 89 associated proteins and P = 2.88 × 10−16, degradation of the extracellular matrix with 41 associated proteins and P = 1.11 × 10−13, calcium ion binding with 56 associated proteins and P = 7.27 × 10−11, and carboxylic acid binding with 30 associated proteins and P = 1.94 × 10−8, supporting a quantitatively robust convergence on inflammatory injury, matrix turnover, and metabolic dysregulation. In the following sections, we examine the most significant pathways in greater detail and discuss how their associated protein networks may contribute to the molecular framework of kidney disease.

Overview of the Enriched Pathways

In Table 1, the enriched biological pathways are ranked according to statistical significance, biological coherence, and likely relevance to kidney disease. Among the strongest signals, inflammatory and innate immune pathways are particularly prominent. The most statistically significant example is neutrophil degranulation (REAC:R-HSA-6798695, p = 2.88 × 10−16, associated protein count = 89), while the nuclear factor kappa B transcription factor motif (TF:M00194_1, p = 1.49 × 10−3, associated protein count = 44) also appears among the prioritized terms. Taken together, these enrichments suggest that persistent inflammatory activation may represent a central component of the disease biology. This interpretation is broadly consistent with prior literature indicating that nuclear factor kappa B signaling is linked to cytokine production, leukocyte recruitment, and tissue injury in both chronic and acute kidney disorders, whereas neutrophil granule release has been associated with oxidative stress, endothelial injury, and amplification of renal inflammation.

Building on this inflammatory context, the enrichment pattern also extends to pathways related to extracellular matrix remodeling and tissue injury. In particular, degradation of the extracellular matrix (REAC:R-HSA-1474228, p = 1.11 × 10−13, associated protein count = 41) and matrix metalloproteinases (WP:WP129, p = 4.07 × 10−6, associated protein count = 13) are strongly represented. These findings are relevant because abnormal extracellular matrix turnover is a recognized feature of kidney disease progression and may provide a mechanistic link between inflammatory injury and the development of glomerular and tubulointerstitial fibrosis. In a related manner, the enrichment of actin-based cell projection (GO:0098858, p = 2.97 × 10−2, associated protein count = 17) and late endosome (GO:0005770, p = 4.55 × 10−2, associated protein count = 24) points to possible alterations in cytoskeletal organization and vesicular trafficking. These processes are important for podocyte structure, tubular transport, and protein handling in the kidney, and their appearance here may therefore fit with the broader pattern of tissue injury and structural disruption.

Extending from structural injury to cellular homeostasis, several metabolic and molecular function terms further indicate possible disturbances in lipid and ion handling. These include carboxylic acid binding (GO:0031406, p = 1.94 × 10−8, associated protein count = 30), calcium ion binding (GO:0005509, p = 7.27 × 10−11, associated protein count = 56), phosphatidylcholine metabolic process (GO:0046470, p = 5.70 × 10−4, associated protein count = 13), and fatty acid transport (GO:0015908, p = 4.18 × 10−4, associated protein count = 16). Although these terms are functionally diverse, together they may be compatible with published observations suggesting that calcium dysregulation, lipid metabolic stress, and altered fatty acid handling contribute to tubular cell injury, mitochondrial dysfunction, and progressive fibrosis. Finally, disease-oriented enrichments such as rheumatoid arthritis (KEGG:05323, p = 6.08 × 10−8, associated protein count = 25), malaria (KEGG:05144, p = 1.15 × 10−7, associated protein count = 18), amyloidosis (HP:0011034, p = 2.82 × 10−2, associated protein count = 12), and acute ischemic stroke (HP:0002140, p = 3.55 × 10−2, associated protein count = 10) may reflect shared mechanisms involving systemic inflammation, vascular injury, and protein deposition that could also be relevant to renal pathology. Overall, the most significant pathways appear to converge on inflammation, extracellular matrix degradation, cytoskeletal and endosomal dysfunction, and metabolic stress, all of which are broadly supported in the literature as contributors to kidney injury and disease progression.

Nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells is a central transcriptional regulatory pathway that coordinates cellular responses to inflammatory, infectious, oxidative, metabolic, and hemodynamic stress. The pathway is typically activated by extracellular signals such as tumor necrosis factor, interleukin one, toll-like receptor ligands, advanced glycation end products, angiotensin two, reactive oxygen species, and mechanical injury. In resting cells, nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells dimers are retained in the cytoplasm through association with inhibitory proteins of the inhibitor of kappa B family. Upon stimulation, inhibitor of kappa B kinase complexes phosphorylate inhibitor of kappa B proteins, promoting their degradation and allowing nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells to translocate to the nucleus, where it binds cognate deoxyribonucleic acid motifs and induces transcription of genes involved in cytokine production, chemokine expression, leukocyte recruitment, cell survival, adhesion, fibrosis, and innate immune activation. The motif provided here, NGGGGAMTTTCCNN, is consistent with a canonical nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells binding sequence and therefore represents a cis-regulatory element through which this pathway can directly control target gene expression. In the kidney, nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells activity is observed in glomerular cells, tubular epithelial cells, endothelial cells, mesangial cells, fibroblasts, and infiltrating immune cells, placing this pathway at the intersection of inflammation, tissue injury, maladaptive repair, and chronic fibrotic remodeling.
