Introduction

Stroke (Disease code: I9_STROKE) is an acute cerebrovascular disorder characterized by the interruption of cerebral blood flow or rupture of cerebral vessels, resulting in rapid neurological injury through ischemia, hemorrhage, inflammation, and tissue remodeling. In an analysis of the UK Biobank Pharma Proteomics Project data, circulating levels of 175 plasma proteins were significantly associated with stroke, based on a cohort of 50,797 controls and 1,392 cases, with significance determined using Bonferroni correction at a threshold of P < 0.000000001. Investigating these proteins is crucial for understanding the molecular underpinnings of stroke, as the disease involves not only vascular events but also systemic responses such as immune activation, extracellular matrix injury, cytokine signaling, and cellular stress adaptation. A comprehensive functional enrichment analysis was conducted on these proteins using pathway databases including Gene Ontology Molecular Function, Gene Ontology Biological Process, Gene Ontology Cellular Component, Reactome, and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes. This analysis identified 70 significantly enriched pathways, highlighting key biological themes such as inflammatory and immune regulation, extracellular matrix and tissue barrier processes, and stress-response signaling. Notable pathways include cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, regulation of leukocyte activation, and extracellular matrix structural constituent, underscoring the roles of cytokine signaling, leukocyte activation, and neurovascular structural remodeling in stroke biology. These findings suggest an integrated molecular framework for stroke, involving cytokine signaling, extracellular structural disruption, and adaptive stress signaling. The following sections will delve into the most significant pathways and their associated protein interactions in greater detail.

    Key Enriched Pathways (Top 10)

    1. HRD1 complex (CORUM:6859)

    Agent Summary: The HRD1 complex is a crucial component of the endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD) pathway, responsible for maintaining protein quality control by identifying and degrading misfolded proteins. Key proteins: LTBR.
    Read Context ▽

    The HRD1 complex is a crucial component of the endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD) pathway, responsible for maintaining protein quality control by identifying and degrading misfolded proteins. This complex, organized around the ubiquitin ligase HMG CoA reductase degradation protein 1, works with accessory factors to facilitate substrate recognition and degradation efficiency. In the context of stroke, the HRD1 complex is relevant due to its role in managing protein misfolding and cellular stress, which are exacerbated during ischemic and reperfusion conditions. Disruption of this pathway can lead to the accumulation of misfolded proteins, increased cellular stress, and neurodegeneration, highlighting its importance in neural tissue viability. Recent studies suggest that enhancing the function of this pathway could mitigate stroke-induced damage by improving endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis and reducing stress.

    2. antigen processing and presentation of peptide antigen via MHC class I (GO:0002474)

    The antigen processing and presentation pathway via MHC class I is crucial for immune surveillance, where intracellular proteins are degraded into peptides, loaded onto MHC class I molecules, and presented on the cell surface for recognition by CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes. This process involves proteasomal degradation, peptide transport into the endoplasmic reticulum, and assembly with MHC class I molecules before being displayed on the plasma membrane. The pathway is regulated by inflammatory signals and can be influenced by infections and cellular stress, which may alter peptide presentation. In the context of stroke, this pathway is relevant due to the sterile inflammation and immune activation that occur, potentially enhancing MHC class I antigen presentation in response to neuronal and vascular cell stress. This may lead to increased recognition of self-peptides by cytotoxic T lymphocytes, potentially exacerbating post-stroke immune responses.

    The pathway "Appendix; goblet cells[≥Low]" highlights the role of goblet cells in maintaining the intestinal barrier, which is crucial for immune homeostasis and epithelial integrity. In the context of stroke, the disruption of this barrier can exacerbate systemic inflammation, linking the intestinal mucosal defense to stroke pathophysiology. The enrichment of 20 stroke-associated proteins in this pathway suggests a significant connection between stroke biology and the gut-associated epithelial defense mechanism, emphasizing the role of mucin-producing goblet cells. Among these proteins, BSG is particularly relevant due to its involvement in maintaining axonal integrity and neuronal survival, which are critical in the aftermath of ischemic injury. Additionally, COL18A1's role in vascular remodeling and angiogenesis further underscores its importance in tissue recovery post-stroke.

    4. endopeptidase inhibitor activity (GO:0004866)

    Agent Summary: Endopeptidase inhibitor activity involves the suppression of endopeptidases, which are enzymes that cleave peptide bonds and play roles in coagulation, fibrinolysis, and inflammation. Key proteins: AMBP, BST2, CST3.
    Read Context ▽

    Endopeptidase inhibitor activity involves the suppression of endopeptidases, which are enzymes that cleave peptide bonds and play roles in coagulation, fibrinolysis, and inflammation. In the context of stroke, this activity is crucial as it regulates enzymes like thrombin and tissue plasminogen activator, which are central to thrombus formation and vascular integrity. The balance of endopeptidase inhibition is vital; insufficient inhibition can lead to thrombosis and neuroinflammation, while excessive inhibition may impair thrombus resolution. Therapeutic inhibitors, including monoclonal antibodies and selective metalloproteinase inhibitors, are used to modulate this activity, impacting stroke prevention and acute brain injury management. Evidence suggests that while targeting coagulation proteases is important for stroke prevention, the efficacy-safety balance must be carefully managed, as seen in trials with inhibitors like asundexian and abelacimab.

    The 'Colon; goblet cells[High]' pathway highlights the specialized role of colonic goblet cells in maintaining the intestinal mucus barrier, which is crucial for epithelial protection and regulating host-microbiota interactions. Disruption of these goblet cell populations can lead to increased gut permeability and systemic inflammation, which may have implications beyond the intestine, including potential impacts on stroke pathobiology. The enrichment of 13 stroke-associated proteins in this pathway suggests a connection between intestinal barrier integrity and stroke, as these proteins are involved in maintaining mucus barrier functions and immune homeostasis. Among these proteins, BSG is particularly relevant to stroke due to its roles in axonal integrity, neuronal survival, and regenerative plasticity, which are critical for recovery post-stroke. However, BSG also acts as a negative regulator of neurite outgrowth, indicating a complex role in both protecting neural structures and potentially limiting regenerative processes after stroke.

    6. MAPK signaling pathway (KEGG:04010)

    Agent Summary: The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway is a crucial intracellular network that translates external stress signals into cellular responses, affecting processes like cell survival, inflammation, and oxidative stress. Key proteins: CD14.
    Read Context ▽

    The mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway is a crucial intracellular network that translates external stress signals into cellular responses, affecting processes like cell survival, inflammation, and oxidative stress. In the context of stroke, particularly ischemic stroke, the MAPK pathway becomes overactivated, leading to increased oxidative stress, mitochondrial damage, inflammatory cytokine production, and neuronal apoptosis, all of which exacerbate brain injury and cognitive deficits. Experimental interventions that suppress this pathway have shown neuroprotective effects, reducing infarct size and improving neurological outcomes. MicroRNAs and long non-coding RNAs play significant roles in modulating MAPK signaling, with some promoting and others inhibiting its activity, thereby influencing stroke outcomes. Additionally, the pathway impacts immune and glial responses post-stroke, further contributing to the complexity of stroke pathobiology and highlighting potential therapeutic targets.

    7. Interleukin-10 signaling (REAC:R-HSA-6783783)

    Agent Summary: Interleukin-10 signaling is a crucial anti-inflammatory pathway that modulates immune responses and aids in tissue protection and repair, particularly relevant in the context of stroke. Key proteins: IL10RB.
    Read Context ▽

    Interleukin-10 signaling is a crucial anti-inflammatory pathway that modulates immune responses and aids in tissue protection and repair, particularly relevant in the context of stroke. This pathway is activated when interleukin-10 binds to its receptor, triggering intracellular signaling that suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines and promotes a reparative immune phenotype. In stroke, interleukin-10 signaling helps reduce secondary inflammatory damage, modulate immune cell activity, and support tissue preservation, particularly in the brain's microglia and macrophages. Experimental evidence shows that interleukin-10 can improve outcomes after stroke by controlling harmful immune responses and protecting glial cells, thus preserving myelin integrity and cognitive function. This pathway's therapeutic potential in stroke recovery is significant, though its efficacy depends on timing, cellular context, and the balance between neuroprotection and systemic immunosuppression.

    8. Matrix metalloproteinases (WP:WP129)

    Agent Summary: Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are zinc-dependent enzymes that play a crucial role in remodeling the extracellular matrix and regulating the blood-brain barrier's integrity, particularly in the context of stroke. Key proteins: BSG, TNF.
    Read Context ▽

    Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) are zinc-dependent enzymes that play a crucial role in remodeling the extracellular matrix and regulating the blood-brain barrier's integrity, particularly in the context of stroke. In the acute phase of ischemic stroke, heightened activity of MMPs, especially MMP-9 and MMP-2, leads to degradation of the vascular basement membrane and disruption of tight junction proteins, which increases blood-brain barrier permeability and facilitates leukocyte infiltration, edema, and hemorrhagic transformation. This activity is linked to complications such as infarct expansion and worsened outcomes, especially when combined with tissue plasminogen activator treatment. However, during the subacute and recovery phases, MMP activity can be beneficial, aiding in tissue remodeling, angiogenesis, and cortical plasticity, with MMP-2 contributing to increased vascular density and recovery. The dual role of MMPs in stroke highlights the importance of tightly regulated MMP activity to balance protective and destructive effects, suggesting that targeted modulation of this pathway could be a therapeutic strategy.

    9. extracellular matrix structural constituent (GO:0005201)

    Agent Summary: The extracellular matrix structural constituent pathway involves proteins and proteoglycans that maintain the architecture and mechanical integrity of tissues, particularly in the brain and vasculature. Key proteins: CD4, CHI3L1, EFEMP1.
    Read Context ▽

    The extracellular matrix structural constituent pathway involves proteins and proteoglycans that maintain the architecture and mechanical integrity of tissues, particularly in the brain and vasculature. These components, such as collagen type four, laminin, and fibronectin, play active roles in regulating cell adhesion, maintaining the blood-brain barrier, and influencing synaptic and cellular plasticity. In the context of stroke, disruptions in this pathway can affect vascular wall stability, blood-brain barrier integrity, and the interactions among various cell types, contributing to stroke risk and influencing the extent of tissue injury and repair. Alterations in extracellular matrix constituents are linked to thromboembolic conditions, cerebral small vessel disease, and ischemic stroke, highlighting their importance in disease progression and recovery. The identification of 14 stroke-associated proteins enriched in this pathway underscores its critical role in stroke susceptibility and the molecular alterations associated with cerebrovascular events.

    10. Pleural mesothelioma (WP:WP5087)

    Agent Summary: Pleural mesothelioma is a malignant disease characterized by chronic inflammation, immune remodeling, and significant heterogeneity in tumor suppressor programs and cellular morphology. Key proteins: ACTA2, MDK.
    Read Context ▽

    Pleural mesothelioma is a malignant disease characterized by chronic inflammation, immune remodeling, and significant heterogeneity in tumor suppressor programs and cellular morphology. The disease creates a systemic prothrombotic and inflammatory state, which can lead to complications such as acute ischemic stroke due to thrombosis and vascular injury. This relationship is supported by evidence of mesothelioma's role in promoting platelet activation and coagulation imbalance through persistent inflammatory signaling. Treatments for pleural mesothelioma, including surgery and systemic therapies, may further influence stroke risk by causing cardiopulmonary toxicity and vascular complications. Overall, the connection between pleural mesothelioma and stroke is complex, involving both disease-driven and treatment-related factors.
